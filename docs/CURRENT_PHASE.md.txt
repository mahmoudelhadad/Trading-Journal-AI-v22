## Objective

Make the Replay chart the primary working surface for a simulated session: show
what the session already recorded — where each execution happened, where the
average entry sits and where the stop is anchored — and allow the actions that
drive that session to be taken from the chart, on the released execution,
persistence and market-data contracts and with no migration.

## Delivered Scope

### Chart visualization

- Execution markers for the session's own executions.
- Weighted average-entry (AVG) price line that moves with the open position.
- Immutable stop (STOP) price line for the open episode's anchored stop.
- Final-exit line cleanup once an episode returns to Flat.
- Overlays isolated per session and cleared on leaving a session or selecting a
  series with no local observations.

### Trading from the chart

- Quick Trade market actions for the current position state, driven entirely by
  the released execution path.
- Order Quantity shared between the chart controls and the detailed Trading
  Panel, so the two surfaces cannot disagree.
- Initial Stop shared while FLAT; once an episode is open its stop is immutable
  and inherited from the open aggregate.
- Exit All for the remaining quantity.

### Replay chart context menu

- Safe right-click menu with Copy Price, Use as Initial Stop and Go To This Bar.

### Navigation

- Step Forward, and Step Backward to the immediately previous EXISTING canonical
  1-minute bar, independent of the displayed timeframe.
- Select Bar navigation to a clicked rendered bar.
- Navigation-only Undo / Redo over cursor and display timeframe, with an
  explicit Play start captured as a history entry and playback frames never
  captured.

### Workspace

- Focus Mode preserving session, position, cursor, timeframe and form state, and
  hiding rather than unmounting the detailed panel.
- Toolbar that can be docked or floated and dragged by an explicit handle, with
  a forced-dock fallback below 768 CSS px and a shared safe top inset that keeps
  a floating toolbar clear of the application header and the Quick Trade strip.
- Compact icon transport controls for Play, Pause, Step Forward, Step Backward,
  Undo and Redo, each carrying its exact hover title and accessible name.

## Runtime Acceptance

Browser Runtime Acceptance is COMPLETE, across narrow, intermediate and desktop
viewports, against a real authenticated account and a real imported 1-minute
series.

Two release-quality defects were found during acceptance and corrected before
this release:

- Focus stacking: the released sticky application header was painted over the
  top of the focused chart and made the Quick Trade strip non-interactive. Focus
  now owns a stacking level above the header while remaining below the user
  menu, modal and confirmation layers. Re-acceptance confirmed by direct
  hit-testing that the Quick Trade controls resolve to themselves.
- Floating-toolbar position: a newly floated toolbar opened inside the header
  band, leaving its drag handle unreachable outside Focus and covering the Quick
  Trade strip inside Focus. Initial position and drag clamp now derive their
  minimum Y from one shared safe-top authority. Re-acceptance confirmed the
  handle is fully reachable and Quick Trade remains uncovered in Focus.

Validated in a normal external browser: native hover tooltips on all six
transport controls with exact labels; the Replay context menu inside Focus; and
two-stage Escape, where the first Escape closes the context menu only and the
second exits Focus.

A single non-reproducible observation of an active session appearing to drop
after a Select Bar navigation did not reproduce across three directed retries,
with the session retained and listed every time. It is carried as an observation
and is not classified as a defect.

## Preserved Contracts

No schema change and no persistence migration is introduced. `schemaVersion`
stays 1 and `actionVersion` stays 1. Sessions written by released v1.9 remain
readable without migration.

Execution authority remains `sessions.enter`, `sessions.exit` and
`sessions.complete`; no execution fill, action identifier or trade identifier is
constructed in the UI. Undo and Redo are navigation-only and can never remove or
alter a simulated trade.

The canonical time architecture is preserved: UTC internally and for the replay
clock, with America/New_York session and display semantics under IANA rules. The
canonical market-data architecture is preserved: 1-minute OHLCV remains
canonical, higher timeframes remain derived, and there is no synthetic gap
filling and no look-ahead.

Journal trades, accounts and storage remain isolated from Replay sessions. The
historical market-data domain remains a separate read-only reconstructable
cache, and the read-only broker-integration policy is unchanged. Unchanged:
Journal IndexedDB schema and LocalStorage contracts; Backup, Restore and
Recovery Bin behavior; account/trade models; Supabase and sync; historical
market-data schema and mutation contracts; the B2b session lifecycle and B2c
scaling and aggregate contracts; and the released application header, its
stacking level and the shared UI button component.

B2d does not include pending orders — Buy Limit, Sell Limit, Buy Stop or Sell
Stop — and introduces no pending entity, pending persistence, trigger engine,
cancellation, modification, pending chart line or limit/stop fill semantics.
Those remain a separate future initiative. B2d also does not include Journal
promotion or trade provenance promotion into the Journal; cloud or multi-device
Backtest session sync; broker execution or order routing; brackets or OCO;
automatic stop execution; automatic take-profit lifecycle; trailing or otherwise
modifiable stops; reversal; hedging; multiple independent simultaneous
positions; fractional contracts; commission or slippage modeling; configurable
fills; durable unrealized P/L or market marks; or persistence redesign.
